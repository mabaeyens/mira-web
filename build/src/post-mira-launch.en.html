A few weeks ago I named my project: Mira

Jul 2026 · First published on LinkedIn, in Spanish

What is Mira? My vision of how to run your own local AI on your own hardware, with full control of the environment. Mira is mostly Python. The apps are Swift for iOS and macOS.

It is also a native inference engine for Apple Silicon and its MLX framework, which takes advantage of the neural accelerators in the M5 chips (M5 only, M1 to M4 do not have them). It has its own small RAG with a reranker, reads files and images, and has a fast mode and a thinking mode. ~60 tok/s, almost 0s ttft. Tested with 32 GB RAM.

It has a command line, a web interface and a mobile app, to use your local model wherever you are, privately and securely. It is open, public and free. There is a beta for iOS and iPadOS, if you are interested.

Why "Mira"? It is a nod to my love of space (the double star Mira, in the constellation Cetus) and because it is the imperative of the Spanish verb "mirar", to look.

Why yet another one of these now? Because it marks a milestone for me in discovering and using artificial intelligence tools, and because it cuts through the huge hype around this technology. The journey began in January 2024 and continues today.

I will not fool you either: since everyone talks about "AI", I want to know what I am talking about from first-hand experience.

Can anyone use Mira? Almost. Right now you need a minimal grasp of uv and Python, although the instructions are very clear. But it will come.

Since it is public and free, what do I get out of it? I think the best way to learn is to build, just as the best way to learn a language is immersion. There is no other way. Theory is fine up to a point, then you have to roll up your sleeves and break things.

How did I make Mira? I used Claude Code heavily, and Mistral, a bit of Qwen and little Gemma, on my own laptop. I put hours into it, weekends and a few of these hot 35-degree nights.

It is one more proof that artificial intelligence is changing the way we work, making us more productive, automating processes and creating new challenges and problems. I have read and learned, and soaked up the work of a few people who care about explaining their content well.

(Surprisingly, this whole post is handwritten with no AI of any kind.)

It would sound fabulous to say I did it for you, to give back to the community or because I love free and open software. The truth is I did it for myself, "just to try".

And the experience has been (is being) very rewarding, and it helps me realise how much I still have to learn. No, seriously. Now I can half understand "Attention is All You Need" from 2017, but my algebra, vectors, matrices and programming were left behind long ago.

I already told you how thrilled I was by my first BASIC program.

That same thrill is why I am publishing this today.
