Promises and results

Apr 2026 · First published on LinkedIn, in Spanish

In the first quarter of 2026, the tech sector laid off 80,000 people, mainly in the US. The official reason, in most cases, was the same: artificial intelligence. It is the first time in history that the Challenger Report, which has been analysing corporate layoffs in the United States for decades, lists AI as the number one cause.

The most talked-about case: Oracle. 30,000 employees affected, 18% of its global workforce. At that moment the company had $523 billion in signed contracts pending delivery, 54% growth in its cloud business, and a net profit that nearly doubled the previous year's. The message to employees said "broader organisational change." What Oracle needed was cash: between 8 and 10 billion dollars to fund 156 billion in AI data centre infrastructure. Note that they did not lay people off because an AI project in production was making those people's work redundant. They did it to pay for the infrastructure where that AI, someday, might run.

Meanwhile, Gartner published in April 2026 that only 28% of AI projects in business operations meet their return expectations. The RAND Corporation puts at 80% the AI projects that do not reach production or do not deliver the promised value. JP Morgan estimates that AI needs to generate more than 600 billion dollars in annual revenue just to justify a 10% return on what has been invested in infrastructure. Goldman Sachs described AI's impact on the economy in 2025 as "basically zero."

That language models and AI work, nobody disputes. Just as the electric motor works. The problem is not the technology, it is what lies underneath. To deploy electric cars at scale you need a charging network that most countries do not have. To deploy AI at scale in a company you need a data layer that most organisations do not have either. Clean, accessible data, with shared definitions, with clear owners.

First the challenge was having lots of business data. Then being able to analyse it. Now it is having it ready so AI can do something useful with it.

AI is not going away. But between the promise and the results there is more work than it seems.
