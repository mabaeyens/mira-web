Strawberries and AI: the r's in strawberry

Mar 2026 · First published on LinkedIn, in Spanish

How many R's are there in "strawberry"?

Most LLMs got it wrong. Two r's, or one, or they just made up the answer.

And then some people throw their hands up: "this AI thing...!"

Well, no. The AI is doing exactly what it was designed to do.

A large language model, or LLM, the AI behind ChatGPT and Claude and Microsoft Copilot and [insert your corporate AI model here], never sees the word "strawberry" the way you do, letter by letter. It sees it as a sequence of fragments, called tokens, that do not necessarily match letters or syllables.

An LLM chops "strawberry" into fragments, turns them into numbers, and works with those numbers. It never sees letters.

Counting letters requires seeing the text as a string of characters. An LLM does not do that. It was not designed for that. It was designed to predict text, and at that it is extraordinary.

The strawberry r's meme goes viral because very few people ask the question that matters: what is an LLM good for, for me? Or put another way: what is this good for in my business, with my data, to sell better or spend less?

AI is not magic. It happened with big data, it happened with blockchain, and it is happening again: the urgency to implement something without knowing where it fits or what problem it solves.

It is a powerful tool, but some problems need a calculator, or a database, or a well-governed data model.

An LLM only knows how to talk. Give it access to your data, a calculator, your systems or an analytics engine, and it stops being a toy and becomes a tool. But for that you need to know what you have, where you have it and what you want to achieve.

If you do not know, AI will not save you. It will repeat your mistakes faster than you can fix them.

An LLM knows how to generate text from text. That is what it is.

What you do with that is up to you.
