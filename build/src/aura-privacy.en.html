Aura · Privacy

Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 August 2026

Aura is a personal weather app for Spain. This policy is short because Aura collects almost nothing, and none of it reaches me.

  • No account. No sign-up. No tracking. No analytics. No ads.
  • No servers of mine. Aura talks only to Spain's official public data services for the weather, and to public open-data sources*. It never phones home to me, because there is no “Aura cloud”.
  • Your location is matched to your municipality entirely on your device. Only that municipality's public code (never your actual position) is sent to the official services to fetch its forecast. It is never linked to your identity, because there is no identity: Aura has no accounts.
  • It's open source, so you can check every word of this for yourself: aura-apps (iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch) and aura-android.

What data Aura collects

None that comes to me. Aura has no backend of mine, no analytics SDK, and no advertising. I don't receive, store, or sell any of your data. I couldn't if I wanted to.

Your location

If you grant location access (“While Using the App”), Aura takes a rough, kilometre-level fix and, on your device, matches it to the nearest Spanish municipality from a list bundled in the app, the same list you can pick from by hand. Only public identifiers then leave your device: that municipality's official code (its INE code) is sent to AEMET, the code of the nearest air-quality station to MITECO, and that municipality's centroid coordinates to Open-Meteo for the hourly UV index, to fetch the forecast, the air reading, and the UV. Your actual coordinates never leave your device, the request carries no name, account, or device identifier that ties it to you, and Aura keeps no history of where you've been.

You can decline location access and Aura still works; you just choose the place yourself.

The services Aura talks to

  • AEMET OpenData, the Spanish State Meteorological Agency (Agencia Estatal de Meteorología), for forecasts, warnings, and UV. opendata.aemet.es
  • MITECO, the Ministry for the Ecological Transition, for air-quality (ICA) readings. miteco.gob.es
  • Open-Meteo, an open European weather API that serves the EU's Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) data, for the hourly UV-index forecast. Only the selected municipality's centroid coordinates are sent, never your device's actual position, and the request carries no key, account, or identifier. open-meteo.com

Your use of the data those agencies provide is governed by their own terms. Aura sends them only what's needed to answer “what's the weather where I am” and reads the answer back.

What actually leaves your device

Because the match happens on your device, each outbound request carries only an identifier or a town centroid, never your GPS position. Here is the exact user data each one sends, with Madrid as the example: 

// AEMET, a municipality ID, not a zip code: the ID AEMET keys its forecasts by.
28079

// MITECO, the nearest air-quality station's ID (plus the day). No coordinates.
28079004#…

// CAMS / Copernicus (via Open-Meteo), a point near you, not your real position.
latitude=XX&longitude=YY

Google Play Data safety

On Android, Google Play shows a Data safety section for every app, and Aura's is as small as it can honestly be. Aura declares that it collects one thing, your approximate location, the municipality, and only to fetch the forecast. That location is processed ephemerally: it is used in memory to answer the request and never stored by me, because there is no server to store it on. It is not shared: sending the place you picked to a weather service is a user-initiated action you'd expect. All of it travels over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. Nothing else, no personal info, no identifiers, no analytics, no ads, is collected or shared, because none of it exists in the app. The same is true of the App Store's privacy label on iOS.

Tips and purchases

Aura has an optional tip jar, and nothing is locked behind it, the app is identical whether or not you ever tip. Payments go through each platform's own billing (Apple's In-App Purchases on iOS, Google Play Billing on Android), which collects the payment details directly from you under that platform's terms. Aura never sees your card or bank details, and it keeps no purchase history off your device. No financial information reaches me.

Your data and your GDPR rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access or delete the personal data an app holds about you. With Aura there is almost nothing to act on, by design: I run no server and keep no copy of anyone's data. An access request returns nothing I hold. To delete everything Aura stores, uninstall the app or clear its storage, that removes the only copy, which lives entirely on your device. If you want to ask anything about this, the contact below is the place, and I'm the only person who reads it.

Data that stays on your device

Cached forecasts, your chosen background theme, and last-used settings live on your device. On iPhone and iPad, if iCloud is available they may sync across your own devices under your Apple ID, and nowhere else. On Android they stay on the phone. Either way, never to me.

Children's privacy

Aura is a general-audience weather app. It does not knowingly collect any data from anyone, children included.
