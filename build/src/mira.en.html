Mira · In beta · Mac, iPhone, iPad

A real assistant. All on your own devices.

Mira is a finished app for Mac, iPhone and iPad. The Mac runs the models, and your iPhone and iPad connect to it over your own private network, so nothing goes through anyone else's servers.

Install the Mac app, scan one QR code with your phone, and there's nothing else to wire up. The others are servers you bolt onto your tools. This one you just open.

What you can do

Chat with your codebase

Open a project folder and ask questions about structure, logic, or specific files.

Summarize any document

Drop in a PDF, an article, a note. Mira reads it locally and nothing leaves the device.

Switch models on the fly

Qwen3.6, Gemma4, Llama, and more, sized to fit any Apple Silicon Mac.

Search the web with context

Grounded answers when a question needs current information.

Pick up on your iPhone

Your conversations live on your Mac and show up on every device you pair. Your phone keeps the list in your own iCloud, so you can see your titles when the Mac is asleep. Opening one needs the Mac.

Run without internet

Full inference offline, zero API keys, no metered costs.

Under the hood

Local inference

Powered by Apple Silicon. Run Qwen3.6, Gemma4, Llama and other open models fully offline. Works on any M-series Mac with 16 GB or more.

No cloud

Your conversations stay on your own devices. The phone talks to your Mac directly, keeps your conversation list in your personal iCloud and its pairing token in your iCloud Keychain. There are no Mira servers. The only third parties are ones you already use: your iCloud, Tailscale for the connection, and the search engines your Mac asks when Mira looks something up.

macOS, iOS and iPadOS

Native SwiftUI apps sharing one backend. The Mac runs the model and a menu-bar app keeps it running. Your iPhone and iPad connect to it over your own Tailscale network, paired with a QR code.

Open source

The inference backend, mira-core, is MIT licensed. Inspect it, modify it, self-host it. You can read exactly what runs on your machine.

Learning by building

It started with a let-down. I downloaded a small model, ran it locally, and asked it to rate my Mac for running AI. It had nothing. Its training stopped in April 2024. So on its own the model was close to useless, and I could see how much I still didn't know. I opened Claude Code and asked, more or less: what do I do with this?

The answer was tools. My first web search worked and then immediately didn't: it kept handing me link names and privacy policies instead of the actual pages. Another letdown. So I asked for a fetch function to go read them, and that one stuck. Suddenly I could ask almost anything, all on my own hardware, and I felt two things at once: how little I still knew, and how good it was to take one more step anyway.

AI is here, and it's useful. But you still need to know what it's good for. It won't replace your judgment. Do your own research.
